# Pedro Cortes Landing Page Generator

A specialized skill for drafting, structuring, and auditing B2B SaaS landing pages using Pedro Cortes's conversion optimization framework, positioning principles, and teardown methodology.

## Summary

This skill translates complex SaaS product capabilities into a clear, outcome-focused narrative that guides cold visitors toward conversion. It enforces the three pillars of conversion (Results, Perceived Superiority, and Risk Mitigation), replaces empty jargon with tangible proof, and structures every page around the proven 9-section blueprint.

## When to Use

Use this skill whenever the user:

- Requests a SaaS landing page, homepage copy, or conversion-focused web blueprint.
- Mentions Pedro Cortes, Cortes Design, or SaaS landing page teardowns.
- Asks to critique, audit, or rewrite an underperforming B2B SaaS landing page.
- Wants to improve hero section conversion, headline clarity, or CTA click-through rates.
- Needs to position a new software product, tool, or service against existing competitors or manual status-quo workflows.

## The Three Pillars of Conversion

Every high-converting SaaS page balances three pillars:

1. **Results (Value Promise):** Articulate the tangible business outcome, not just software features.
2. **Perceived Superiority:** Explicitly contrast why this approach beats direct competitors and the status quo (manual spreadsheets, duct-taped tools).
3. **Risk Mitigation:** Lower switching friction, onboarding anxiety, and commitment risk so taking action feels effortless.

## Core Conversion Principles

1. **Hero "Aha Moment" Over Feature Dumps:** Spark curiosity and demonstrate transformation in the hero rather than listing features or showing static dashboards.
2. **Call to Value (CTV) Over Call to Action (CTA):** Replace generic directives ("Sign Up", "Book a Demo") with the tangible outcome ("Book a 30-Minute Demo and Get 5 Tailored Insights", "Start Free Trial - See Your First Report in 5 Minutes").
3. **Sell Outcomes Indirectly:** Avoid unbelievable claims ("10x your revenue!"). Detail the operational efficiency so clearly that the visitor reaches the ROI conclusion themselves.
4. **The Power of Contrast:** Contrast the chaotic current reality (manual spreadsheets, lost leads, endless syncs) against the streamlined new reality.
5. **Show vs. Tell:** Ban empty adjectives ("fast", "intuitive", "easy", "AI-powered"). Replace with proof ("Build a 20-email nurture sequence in 12 minutes").
6. **Quantify the Cost of Inaction:** Emphasize the hidden financial or operational cost of not fixing the problem.
7. **The Crucial 20% (80/20 Rule):** Do not dump 40 features on the page. Highlight only the 2 to 4 core capabilities that drive 80% of customer adoption.
8. **Directly Address Switching Friction:** Answer the hidden objection ("Switching tools is painful") by detailing 1-click migration and fast onboarding.
9. **Outcome-Driven Social Proof:** Ditch vague compliments. Use specific, relatable customer proof with exact metrics, timeframes, and matching job titles.
10. **Two-Tier CTA Strategy:** Pair a primary CTV for high-intent buyers with an alternative CTA (interactive tour, calculator, benchmark template) for earlier-stage prospects.

## Workflow: Generating a Landing Page

Follow this sequence to produce landing page copy.

### Step 1: Extract Core Positioning Inputs

Gather or deduce the five essential variables:

1. **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP):** Exact decision maker and team (job title, team size, industry).
2. **Current Pain and Status Quo:** The manual tools, spreadsheets, or broken processes used today, and what inaction costs them.
3. **The "Aha Moment":** The specific visual moment or quick win that demonstrates the product's unique value.
4. **The Crucial 20%:** The 2 to 4 primary capabilities that produce 80% of the customer's results.
5. **Switching Anxiety:** What makes switching intimidating, and how the product eliminates that barrier.

### Step 2: Write the 9-Section Page Copy

Draft the page section by section in this exact order:

#### 1. Hero Section (Above the Fold)
- **Category / Eyebrow Tag:** State the product category and target audience (e.g., "The Async Standup Tool For Remote Engineering").
- **Pattern-Interrupt Headline:** Use one of Pedro Cortes's core formulas:
  - *Outcome Without Pain:* [Achieve Desired Outcome] Without [Dreaded Pain or Tedious Old Process]
  - *Category Re-positioning:* The [Category / Mechanism] Built Specifically For [Niche ICP] To [Core Benefit]
  - *Problem Reversal:* Stop [Painful Frustration]. Start [Effortless Desirable Outcome].
- **Subheadline (Clarity First):** Explain what the product actually is in plain English, how it works, and who it is for in 1-2 sentences.
- **Primary Call to Value (CTV):** Value-oriented button text (e.g., "Start 14-Day Free Trial - See Your Pipeline Score Today").
- **Risk Reversal Note:** Micro-copy below button ("No credit card required • 2-minute setup • Cancel anytime").
- **Visual "Aha Moment" Spec:** Detailed description of a dynamic visual demonstrating the core transformation (e.g., audio clip evolving into clean slide deck), not abstract art.

#### 2. Initial Social Proof (Authority Strip)
- Display 4 to 6 recognizable customer logos in the target niche, or a verified volume metric (e.g., "$1.2B in pipeline tracked").

#### 3. The Problem and Status Quo (The Contrast)
- Detail the painful reality of the current method (spreadsheets, manual chasing, disconnected tools).
- List the 3 core pain points of the old way and quantify the cost of inaction.

#### 4. The Solution and Mechanism (The Crucial 20%)
- Reverse the problems from Section 3.
- Present the solution as a clean 3-step workflow (Step 1: Connect / Ingest; Step 2: Automated Core Magic; Step 3: Actionable Result).
- Focus only on the 20% of features that deliver 80% of value, complete with UI screenshot descriptions.

#### 5. Reinforcing Benefits and Perceived Superiority
- Answer: "Why pick you over alternatives or doing nothing?"
- Highlight 3 operational outcomes: speed to value, accuracy/reliability, and seamless team adoption.
- Include a contrast table (The Old Way vs. The Product Way).

#### 6. Switching Ease and Friction Removal ("How Easily Can You Switch?")
- Directly address migration fears.
- Specify 1-click import, native integrations with existing tools, and same-day onboarding.

#### 7. Relatable, Outcome-Driven Social Proof
- Provide 2 to 3 testimonials with exact metrics, timeframes, and customer name, job title, and company.
- Focus on quantifiable ROI and smooth adoption.

#### 8. Final Call to Value and Risk Reversal
- Reiterate the core transformation headline.
- Pair the primary CTV button with zero-risk terms.

#### 9. Objection-Busting FAQ and Alternative Low-Friction CTA
- Answer the top 4 objections: setup time, data security, incumbent tool contracts, and pricing tiers.
- Include an Alternative CTA for early-stage visitors (interactive product sandbox, benchmark cheatsheet, or video walkthrough).

## Workflow: Conducting a Teardown Audit

When auditing an existing page, score each component:

1. **Headline Clarity:** Is it an outcome-based pattern interrupt, or vague corporate fluff?
2. **Visual Proof:** Does the hero show the actual product in action ("aha moment"), or generic stock illustrations?
3. **Call to Value:** Does the button promise clear value, or just demand effort ("Submit", "Book Demo")?
4. **Contrast:** Does the page clearly contrast the new solution against the painful status quo?
5. **Switching Friction:** Does the page reassure visitors about how easy migration is?
6. **Social Proof Specificity:** Are testimonials quantified with metrics and matching job titles?

Provide actionable before-and-after copy rewrites for every identified gap.

## Gotchas and Common Pitfalls

- **The Adjective Trap:** Avoid empty words like "fast", "intuitive", "easy", "powerful", "seamless", or "AI-driven". Replace adjectives with concrete operational proof and numbers.
- **The Feature Dump:** Never list 20 features in a grid. Prospects buy solutions to problems, not software feature catalogs.
- **Vague CTAs:** Never use standalone "Learn More", "Submit", or "Get Started". Always state the immediate payoff.
- **Abstract Hero Graphics:** Never recommend abstract 3D shapes or zoomed-out dashboards. Hero visuals must showcase the moment of transformation.
- **Ignoring the Status Quo:** The primary competitor is usually inertia and Excel spreadsheets. Agitate the pain of the status quo.
